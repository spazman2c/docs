title: "Datasets and response views"
description: "Use shorter dataset names and consistent fixture, odds and participant fields without changing existing identities."
---

A dataset selects an isolated identity catalogue and its permitted source reads. The API parameter remains `namespace` for compatibility; the default playground derives it from your bookmaker choice. The **Dataset** selector remains in the advanced explorer.

| Preferred alias | Existing public name |
| --- | --- |
| `sportsbooks` | `oddswire_bookmakers` |
| `regional` | `oddswire_regional` |
| `pilot` | `oddswire_live` |

The short aliases and existing public names remain compatible for REST reads. Account entitlements retain their established namespace values. Aliases grant no features, sources, regions or credits. `pilot` is a dataset name, not a promise of in-play or current coverage. Stream subscriptions accept the documented dataset aliases while preserving dataset scope and exact native identity. Management bodies keep their documented namespace spellings.

## Consistent fixture and odds responses

Existing calls retain their original response shape by default. Add `view=standard` to fixture or odds reads for common `DatasetFixture` and `DatasetOdds` records. Explicit `view=legacy` keeps the original format. Both use the existing `data`, `missing[]` and `meta` envelope, paging, authentication and credit rules.

```text
GET /v1/fixtures?namespace=regional&view=standard
GET /v1/odds?namespace=regional&fixture=YOUR_FIXTURE_ID&view=standard
GET /v1/fixtures?namespace=sportsbooks&source_id=14&mode=retained&view=standard
GET /v1/odds?namespace=sportsbooks&source_id=14&fixture=YOUR_NATIVE_FIXTURE_ID&capture_id=YOUR_CAPTURE_ID&mode=retained&view=standard
```

The capitalized values are placeholders; discover IDs within your approved scope first. For the `sportsbooks` dataset, select exactly one reviewed source using `source_id`. Sources 5/14/15 use the regional reader and require the separate source-native permission; live mode additionally requires the required live-data permission and supports14/15 only. Sources 11/12/13 require `native_odds`. These grants remain separate from canonical `fixtures` and `odds` grants. Unsupported native filters fail instead of silently switching readers. The old `/v1/native/...` routes also accept the standard view.

## Fields and identity

- Fixtures expose `id`, `identity_scope`, optional source/sport/competition IDs, schedule, status, participants and evidence. Unknown metadata stays null or empty; `original` retains the existing full record and bindings.
- Odds expose fixture/source/market/selection IDs, names, class, period, line/unit, side, value, state, participant evidence and verification flags. Stale or suspended prices are not promoted into the common active `value` field. The original record retains its explicit historical/diagnostic state.
- Resource identifiers in the standard view are exact strings, including leading zeros. Registered source IDs and known canonical sport/competition IDs retain their numeric types. Legacy responses keep their original ID types.
- Compare a line together with `line_unit`: canonical `quarter_points` are integer quarter units; native lines retain their documented unit. Equal-looking numbers are not a settlement compatibility decision.
- `evidence` preserves capture, receive, confirmation and source times where available. Retained evidence does not become current when delivered or replayed. `meta.dataset` gives the preferred short dataset name for standard responses; `meta.namespace` retains its established representation.

Common formatting does not create global fixture/player IDs or cross-source market compatibility. Retain dataset, source and identity scope together. Native names alone are not identifiers.

## Participants

```text
GET /v1/participants?namespace=regional&kind=team
GET /v1/participants?namespace=regional&kind=player&fixture=YOUR_FIXTURE_ID
GET /v1/participants?namespace=sportsbooks&source_id=14&kind=player&fixture=YOUR_NATIVE_FIXTURE_ID&capture_id=YOUR_CAPTURE_ID&mode=retained
```

`kind=team` is the default. Canonical participants are restricted to accessible collected fixtures and associated permitted markets; unrelated catalogue rows are excluded. Native teams come from admitted fixture evidence. Native player reads require a fixture and, for regional, its exact capture. Missing player identity returns an explicit missing reason rather than a fabricated ID.

Participant IDs can be null for name-only native evidence. Non-null native IDs keep their provider scope, source and fixture context, and `identity_verified` remains explicit. Names and native IDs are never merged across books. These are bounded observations, not a complete roster or league-wide player catalogue.

## Fixture detail

```text
GET /v1/fixtures/YOUR_FIXTURE_ID?namespace=regional
GET /v1/fixtures/YOUR_NATIVE_FIXTURE_ID?namespace=sportsbooks&source_id=14&mode=retained
```

Detail reads always use the standard view and return a one-record page. Missing or inaccessible fixtures do not fall back to another dataset, source or capture. If multiple retained captures match, the API returns 409 `fixture_ambiguous`; supply the exact `capture_id` instead of selecting an arbitrary historical snapshot. A detail read needs the same underlying feature/source scope as fixture discovery. It does not return scores, settlements or odds, and does not grant those capabilities.

## Choosing the correct reader

Numeric fixture IDs are unique **within a dataset**, not across datasets. Keep
`namespace` with every fixture ID; matching numbers do not establish a shared
match. Native fixture IDs remain scoped to their source. A common response shape
does not certify a cross-source mapping.

Sources 11/12/13 use `/v1/native/fixtures` and `/v1/native/odds`.
Sources 14/15 use `/v1/native/source/fixtures` and `/v1/native/source/odds`;
passing 14/15 to the generic native reader is unsupported. Use the standard
sportsbooks view above to select the reader through `source_id`.

The native review is separate from canonical football/1X2 access. Approved native
scopes can include basketball and provider-labelled props; they do not certify
canonical players, settlement compatibility or a canonical family mapping.

For generic native odds, `market_type` selects an exact returned provider market
type, for example `market_type=MATCH_ODDS`. It does not infer a canonical family.
Cursors remain bound to the returned evidence; a changed snapshot returns 409
rather than combining pages from different books. Narrow before starting paging.

`delivery.price.confirmed_ts` is the latest successful canonical confirmation;
`delivery.price.observation.seen_ts` preserves the last semantic change receipt.
A price can remain unchanged for hours while confirmations keep it fresh. Stale
confirmations never become fresh because a request is served.

For source 11, `MATCH_ODDS` is Match 1X2 (goals), while `BK_MATCH_1X2` is Bookings Match 1X2. Their different prices are not a home/away reversal. Retain the provider market name, type, period and native identity; `market_type=MATCH_ODDS` selects the former.

## Look up a bookmaker by source ID

The free [live catalogue](https://oddswire.io/api/catalogue) provides current names
without an API key or a namespace. Its `document.bookmakers` entries contain
`id`, `name` and `collectorId`. A **numeric** `id` is the native API's `source_id`;
`collectorId` is the website slug. String catalogue IDs are separate identities
and must not be coerced into numeric source IDs.

```bash
curl --fail-with-body --silent --show-error --max-time 10 \
  https://oddswire.io/api/catalogue
```

Read the live revision rather than keeping a compiled name table. Catalogue
membership does not establish current prices, collection, source permissions or
your account's access. The native `sportsbooks` dataset does not yet support the
canonical `/sports`, `/bookmakers` or `/competitions` readers; use the catalogue
for source names and the documented native fixture reader for native identities.


## Reviewed shared fixture IDs

Canonical fixture and odds records may include `shared_fixture` with a stable UUID,
authority epoch, review revision and `swapped` orientation. Existing integer fixture
IDs and native source keys remain unchanged. The current reviewed scope is 66 football
fixtures across the regional and prediction datasets; other fixtures may omit it.
This establishes reviewed fixture identity only, not settlement/price compatibility.

Use `shared_fixture=YOUR_UUID` on `/v1/fixtures` or `/v1/odds`, with an explicit
`namespace` and your permitted books. Do not combine it with `fixture`. The same
UUID can locate different source-local IDs in different reviewed datasets. Access,
bounded pages, freshness and credit rules still apply. An inaccessible/unknown UUID
returns 404; changed review/metadata invalidates a lookup or cursor with 409; an
unavailable identity authority returns 503. Never reuse an old review after a
changed epoch. Broader unreviewed native feeds do not gain shared IDs automatically.

## Native display-label evidence

1xBit native offers now include reviewed English market/outcome templates where the
original group/type/line or player inputs resolve exactly. `label_evidence` retains
the original names, dictionary digest and `resolved`, `market_only` or
`template_unresolved` state. Unknown packed-score/time templates remain unresolved.
Display labels do not change native IDs, price clocks, line units, settlement or
permissions.

20Bet now resolves ordinary native market/outcome labels and missing variant-market
label fallbacks from archived English
dictionaries containing 5,518 market and 3,406 outcome templates. Binding uses
internal dictionary IDs from the original native key, rather than substituting a
vendor ID. The label evidence includes `dictionary_market_id`,
`dictionary_outcome_id` and `market_template` where applicable. Supported literal
participant/number/handicap substitutions preserve the original specifier string;
unknown arithmetic, unbound player tokens and other unsupported templates remain
explicitly unresolved. Existing provider variant/player labels are preserved.
Known 1xBit paired-player templates also resolve when the archived name/parameter
vectors match the original packed line exactly; ambiguous vectors stay unresolved.
No market-family, settlement, canonical player or source-use approval follows.
Do not apply one bookmaker's dictionary to another source.
